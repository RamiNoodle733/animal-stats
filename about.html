THE MODEL

What each battle stat means

Comparative ratings, not laboratory units
01

Attack

Capacity to inflict damage using natural weapons, striking force, bite, venom, constriction, or comparable offensive tools.

02

Defense

Protection and durability from armor, hide, shell, body mass, pain tolerance, and resistance to an opponent’s attacks.

03

Agility

Acceleration, reflexes, turning, balance, and control of movement—not simply top speed.

04

Stamina

Ability to sustain exertion, recover, and keep functioning during a prolonged encounter.

05

Intelligence

Problem solving, learning, tactical flexibility, perception, communication, and useful social coordination.

06

Special

Unusual matchup-changing tools that do not fit cleanly elsewhere, such as venom, electricity, camouflage, echolocation, or regeneration.

WORKFLOW

How a profile is built

  1. Identify the animal.Confirm the common name, scientific name, and intended taxon.
  2. Review factual ranges.Compare credible references for adult size, mass, movement, defenses, natural weapons, behavior, and habitat.
  3. Resolve context.Prefer representative healthy adults and explain sex, age, region, or environment differences when they materially change a matchup.
  4. Assign comparative ratings.Translate the reviewed profile into six 0–100 editorial scores, avoiding double-counting the same advantage across categories.
  5. Version and correct.Publish changes in a numbered release and revise records when stronger evidence or a clear error is found.

BOUNDARIES

What the numbers cannot prove

  • There is no universal scientific “attack” or “special” score for an animal.
  • A single number cannot capture every age, sex, population, terrain, or encounter.
  • Top speed, body mass, and bite-force reports may be estimates or ranges and can conflict.
  • Higher total stats do not guarantee a win; matchup mechanics and environment still matter.
  • The site is an educational powerscaling project, not veterinary, wildlife-handling, or safety advice.

REFERENCE POLICY

How sources are selected

Prefer named, reviewable records over unattributed summaries

These resources are starting points and cross-checks, not a claim that one database settles every field. Individual records should ultimately link to the exact pages, papers, or institutional references used.

Conflict rule

When reputable sources disagree, the profile should show a range or explain the context instead of silently choosing the most dramatic value. Scientific names are cross-checked separately from combat interpretation.

IMAGE STANDARD

Real animals, transparent presentation

Replacement standard

New animal assets must begin with a real photograph of the correct animal. The subject is isolated onto a genuinely transparent background; cartoons, generated animals, 3D renders, watermarked stock previews, and unrelated species are rejected.

Traceability

Approved replacements record the creator, source page, license, asset checksum, dimensions, and review status. Existing legacy art is being replaced progressively rather than being mislabeled as already verified.

QUICK ANSWERS

Common questions

Are the battle scores scientific measurements?

No. Factual fields can be sourced from wildlife references, while the six battle scores are transparent editorial ratings for comparison.

Does the highest total always win?

No. Totals summarize broad capability; size, weapons, environment, behavior, and matchup-specific advantages can change the outcome.

Who created Animal Battle Stats?
